- **Fixed: a live dither field no longer builds up brightness as it runs — a working card, a list strip, the ticket's work strip, a streaming tail, generating blocks, drafting ghosts and the design splash now paint, frame after frame, exactly what a full redraw paints; the five fields that had been seen built up keep that weight from their first frame (N11).** The engine rewrites only the dots whose pixel changes, and it erased a dot over its own fractional rect before refilling it. The fill antialiases into the device pixels it half covers, and the erase did not reach them: Chromium rounds a clear to whole pixels and skips them, so their ink only grew (a working card held about 6 times its own alpha after 5 seconds and 28 times after a minute, its dots swelling into rings); WebKit clears them by coverage, so they settled at 2 to 4 times. The erase now covers every whole device pixel a dot can touch, outside the mask's clip, one erase per run of a row's rewritten dots; where dots share device pixels (cover mode, a page zoomed out) a changing frame is drawn as a full rebuild. The look was not given up: the owner asked for the fix without the fields going faint, so the card wash and trace, the ticket strip and the list strip's wash and trace (`KanbanCard`, `WorkbenchTicker`, `BoardList`) now carry, from the first frame, the ink and visible-dot area they had in HiDPI Chromium between 4 and 6 seconds in (a 2 px dot, with alpha and strength fitted; the table is `LIVE_FIELD_TUNING` in `lib/dither-engine.ts`). The pitch-2 live fields and every idle field kept their numbers: at those positions their dots already sat on whole device pixels and never built up. A ring trace (`DitherBorder`) now paints only the cells its 2 px ring can show (`edgeBand`), and a masked field skips cells its mask hides, with no visible pixel changed, so a board of live cards costs less than before the re-tune. Verified: a parity harness in real Chromium and WebKitGTK 2.52 (headless mutter) drove every live field and three idle ones tick by tick on a hand-stepped clock and compared each against a field rebuilt from nothing at the same frame — at DPR 1, 1.5 and 2, three sub-pixel positions each, 0 px differ in Chromium and in WebKit on CPU-backed canvases (before: 7,783 to 40,000 px and up to 67 times the alpha), and below DPR 1 and in cover mode too; on WebKit's default backend the two masked fields at DPR 1 differ from a rebuild by a level in 700 to 1,400 edge pixels that stay flat from 1 to 30 seconds (rounding of the clip, no drift); the cell skip changes 0 px in the ring or under the mask in both engines; `dither-engine.test.ts` adds a model canvas that rasterises like each engine and fails on the old engine with the same build-up; on a board with two working and two queued cards (Chromium, DPR 1), main thread 83 → 79 ms/s and fillRect + clearRect 43.8k → 36.6k a second, and in the list view 268 → 216 ms/s and 206k → 138k (fillRect alone 102k → 118k, the re-tuned strips rewrite more often); before and after screenshots of every live field kind in both themes and both engines.
- **Changed: a loading skeleton keeps its motion and its pixels, and the page clock sleeps between its 8 Hz ticks instead of running 60 frames a second (UB6).** The skeleton's static re-rolls 8 times a second, but it filtered every frame of the shared page clock to find those 8, so one skeleton cost 60 animation frames a second. `subscribeToClock(fn, everyMs)` in `lib/motion.ts` makes a subscription periodic: it runs on the first frame past each multiple of `everyMs`, and while only periodic subscribers are left the clock sleeps on a timer and wakes one frame early for the boundary. The skeleton subscribes at 125 ms, and walks its mask's cells once per resize instead of once per tick. Every skeleton in the UI is the one `Skeleton` component (`SkeletonRows` and `SkeletonCard` compose it), so this covers them all.
