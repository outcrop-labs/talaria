- **Fixed:** Four agent-plane doors accepted less identity than they acted on. `GET /api/agents/{id}/heartbeat` let an UNNAMED org-wide-key caller read any agent's work queue and stamp its liveness (the cross-check only ran for a caller it could name) — it now refuses with 403 `x-agent-name required`. `POST /api/agents/register` was gated on "holds any fleet key" and upserted by `name`, so any credential could rewrite any agent's role, framework, capabilities and status — the row is now bound to the caller's identity: a `tak_` caller registers only its own model, a legacy caller only the name it asserted (and never a personal or elevated one, which `agent_caller` already refuses), anything else is 403 "this credential belongs to …". `POST /api/agents/cron-output` and `POST /api/tasks/{id}/usage` wrote under `caller.model` without asking whether that identity was proven; both now call `refuse_legacy`, so a shared-key holder naming a non-privileged agent can no longer post a fabricated cron reply into its listening thread or inflate its ticket spend. `GET /api/skills/{owner}/{name}` let any signed-in member read every agent's SKILL.md, a personal assistant's custom prompts included — the shared root and an org agent's skills stay org work material, a personal assistant's are readable by its owner, its managers and fleet-wide readers only. Also: `edit_cron_job` trimmed AFTER its leading-dash guard (a prompt of `" --schedule …"` reached `hermes cron edit` as an option); it trims first now, name and schedule alike, matching `create_cron_job`.
