- **Added: an app can have routes below its surfaces — `/x/<slug>/<anything>` renders the work surface and `/x/<slug>/manage/<anything>` the manage surface.** An app's list, record pages and tabs now have addresses that can be linked, bookmarked, reloaded and stepped through with Back and Forward. Before this, `/x/<slug>/items/42` rendered Not Found. sv-router lets the top-level `*` beat a catch-all nested in the `/` layout group, so the app routes now form their own group under the same shell. The SDK gains `useAppRoute()`, which gives the path below the surface, its decoded segments, the query, and `match('items/:id')` with params typed from the pattern. It also gains `appHref(path, { surface? })` and `appNavigate(path, { surface?, replace? })`, which resolve relative to the surface the page shows; `useApp()` gains `rest` and `query`. A settings tab stays one page with no routes below it, and links elsewhere with `{ surface }`. The route gate, the dock, the manage sidebar and Rust's `ViewGrants::denies` give every route below `/x/<slug>` to the work grant and every route below `/x/<slug>/manage` to the manage grant. They compare paths case-insensitively, as the router does: a work grant used to pass `/x/<slug>/MANAGE`, which the router mounts as the manage surface, and `/X/<slug>` passed as a core view nobody denies. `navigateHref` now passes a query and hash in the router's options instead of in the path, so notification and activity links such as `/artifacts?a=<id>` open their view instead of Not Found. The scaffold's work surface links each item to its own page at `items/<id>`, and its server answers `GET items/<id>`.
