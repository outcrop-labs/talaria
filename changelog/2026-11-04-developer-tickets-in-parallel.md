- **Fixed: a Developer Agent's tickets progress in parallel instead of one at a time behind a single long build.** Talaria and Hermes already ran each ticket's session concurrently. The bottleneck was the agent's own container. Every ticket's clone, build, test suite and Oh My Pi run executes inside it, and a chassis seeded from an older template still gives it `cpus: ${AGENT_CPUS:-2}`, so up to 16 tickets' builds shared two cores. On the owner's instance three tickets ran on those two cores at a load average of 15, on a 32-core host. A cold `cargo build` ran past Hermes' 420-second tool ceiling, was killed and started again from cold, and two of the three tickets spent 40 minutes without reaching their harness. A developer agent's container now gets its own CPU settings instead of the chassis's. Its `cpus` is the host's cores less an eighth kept back for the platform, at least 2 (28 on a 32-core host). It stays a ceiling rather than no limit so that cargo, Go and the JVM, which size their parallelism from the cgroup, do not each assume the whole host. Its `cpu_shares` is 512, half the default, so sixteen builds yield to the app and Postgres under contention. Operators can override both in `fleet/.env` with `AGENT_WB_CPUS` (0 means no limit) and `AGENT_WB_CPU_SHARES`. A chassis `AGENT_CPUS` no longer reaches a developer agent. Its config now sets Hermes' `gateway.api_server.max_concurrent_runs` to the live-job cap plus 8, which is 24. Hermes' default of 10 counts chats and crons too, and would have refused the eleventh ticket's turn with a 429. A def that set its own larger cap, or no cap, keeps it. The Oh My Pi skill now says to run steps that may pass five minutes in the background and wait on them with `process_manage`, never through the foreground ceiling. Each job gets its own `--session-dir`. Applies to any repository a workbench runs. **Existing Developer Agents get this only when they are rolled, which recreates the container.** Upgrading Talaria re-renders their config, but a running container keeps its CPU limit and Hermes reads its run cap only at start. Roll each Developer Agent from Agents (the agent's Roll control, or Roll all), or with `POST /api/fleet/agents/{id}/control` and `{"action":"roll"}`.

  Verified: render unit tests cover the CPU block on outcrop's chassis (`cpus: ${AGENT_CPUS:-2}` and no `cpu_shares`) and on a quota/period chassis. They also cover the core-count default (32 → 28, 4 → 3, 2 → 2) and the run cap: a def's larger cap, or 0, negative or quoted no-cap, stands, and a smaller or junk value is raised. The whole `talaria-fleet-render` suite passes. `docker compose config` with the rendered lines gives `cpus: 4` for `AGENT_WB_CPUS=4` and drops `cpus` for `AGENT_WB_CPUS=0`. With no override, a created container reads `NanoCpus=28000000000`, `CpuShares=512`. Before this follow-up, the first version of the change dropped the quota outright. On a worktree stack whose chassis carries outcrop's `cpus: ${AGENT_CPUS:-2}`, the Developer Agent (Hermes v0.22.4) then came up with `NanoCpus=0` and `max_concurrent_runs: 24` in its config. Three tickets assigned to it were dispatched in the same second, and the agent ran their steps at the same time. Six live tickets then ran a stand-in build (two CPU-bound jobs each) every turn. Uncapped, each build took 8–11 s. After `docker update --cpus 2`, which is the quota the old render kept, the same builds took 46–55 s. Once the quota was lifted they were back to 8–10 s. All 335 of the agent's model calls were booked to their tickets.
