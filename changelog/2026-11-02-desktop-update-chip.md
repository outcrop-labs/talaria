- **Changed: Talaria Desktop tells you when a new version is out. It checks on its own, 30 seconds after launch and then every 6 hours, and shows a small chip, "Talaria Desktop 0.13.0 is available", with Update and Later — on the launcher, and over whichever instance you have open, whatever Talaria version that instance runs. Over an instance, with the themed titlebar (the default), it sits in the middle of the window's top bar, where it covers nothing you type or read; with the OS titlebar or none, it starts as a small pill just below the page's nav, over the middle of its header line. "–" makes it a small pill at any time, even while an update downloads, and the pill keeps counting; a small window or a page zoomed in starts with the pill. Later puts that version off for a day, everywhere at once and across a relaunch; a newer version shows straight away. Update downloads it with its progress showing, checks its signature, installs it and restarts Talaria (on Windows the installer runs on its own and reopens the app); a download that has had nothing arrive for a minute is given up with a sentence and Update is offered again, however slow the connection otherwise is. Where the app cannot replace itself — a Linux .deb, .rpm, pacman or Flatpak install, a Windows .msi install, or a Mac copy still running from its disk image — the chip says how to update instead and offers only Later, and Check for updates no longer offers an Install that could not work (on a .deb or .rpm it downloaded the AppImage and then refused it).** The shell checked only when someone pressed Check for updates on the launcher or in Settings → Profile, so nobody learned about a release. The check is the same bounded one (10 s), off the main thread; a check that fails is only a log line, and tries again an hour later. Update's own look at the feed is bounded at 20 s; the download's only bound besides the minute without bytes is a 4-hour backstop for a download that trickles forever, whose sentence points to github.com/outcrop-labs/talaria/releases. The chip over an instance is the shell's own, put into the page by the shell (it needs nothing from the instance's UI, reappears after a reload, and is never put into a sign-in provider's page); a failed install, or one that panics, shows "↑ Update failed" instead of staying on "Updating", and opening it gives the sentence saying why.
  Verified: on Linux, on a private headless rig (its own mutter and session bus, WebDriver on the launcher), with a test build of the shell (version 0.12.0) reading a fake `latest.json` on localhost and trusting a throwaway signing key. As a package install (no `$APPIMAGE`): the feed was asked 30.4 s after launch and the chip appeared on the launcher with Later only and the package sentence, and in the instance page opened next; `install_update` refused with the same sentence; Later in the page took the chip down in the page and on the launcher and wrote `updateSnooze` for 0.13.0 exactly 24 h out; a manual check of the same version stayed put off; the feed saying 0.13.1 brought the chip back in both views at once; a feed answering 500 left it as it was and said so in a sentence; and a relaunch with the feed on the version put off showed no chip in either view. As an AppImage (`$APPIMAGE` set, as the AppImage runtime sets it), on a page whose `style-src 'self'` refused its own inline styles: the chip sat at [474, 4, 806, 32] in a 1280-wide window, centred, its buttons at 11 px weight 600, styled by its constructed sheet; a payload that stopped after half its bytes was dropped 60.8 s after Update with "The download stopped: nothing arrived for 60 seconds, so nothing was installed. Try Update again.", the AppImage untouched; on a page where a constructed sheet never applies, the chip was styled through CSSOM instead (the same 6 px radius, 11 px buttons); and a 200 KB payload trickled at 10 KB a second showed its progress (4 %, 14 %), was made a pill mid-download ("↑ Updating 29%", then 44 %, 59 %), finished without being cancelled, and replaced and relaunched the AppImage; a signature that did not match said so and installed nothing. Over the real Talaria UI (its own vite dev server, the api mocked, Chromium, the shell's chip script evaluated into the page as the shell does), at 1000 and 1600 px wide, on a channel with a four-line draft in the composer and on a thread in the side panel, with three toasts up: the chip, the installing chip and its pill each sat inside the 36 px titlebar, clear of the window controls, and overlapped no composer, send button or toast. In round 3, over the same real UI in each titlebar mode at 1000 and 1600 px: themed, the chip sat inside the bar and touched no link or button; with the OS titlebar or none, it started as the pill at [466, 62, 535, 87] (1000 px), below the nav, touching no link or button, and opened it sat over the header line, overlapping its star button by 3 px at 1000 px; a failed install folded to "↑ Update failed" in every mode; four progress pushes changed the title in place and kept the same nodes; a 760 × 600 window at 175 % zoom (434 CSS px) started with the pill, and opened, its row fit between the edge and the window controls, the title cut short. On the rig the shell put the chip in the page's top bar (top 4 px) with the themed titlebar, moved it to the pill at 62 px when the launcher switched to the OS titlebar and to none, and back when it switched back. Unit tests cover the snooze (a day, a newer version, a pre-release, an unparseable version, a clock that went back), which copies are offered Update on each OS, the download's bounds (an AppImage at 256 kbit/s is never stopped), progress, an install that panics, the error sentences and the page script. The connect smoke passes on the rig. `bun run gate` passes its check and the ui typecheck and tests (the desktop typecheck passes on its own); its desktop clippy cannot build on this host, which has no GTK headers, so fmt, clippy `-D warnings` and the 145 tests ran in the Fedora 44 rig image. Not verified: Windows (NSIS passive install and relaunch, the .msi case) and macOS (the app's own replace, the disk-image case) at run time; the real GitHub feed; a page whose CSP restricts `script-src` or `connect-src` (only `style-src` was restricted on the rig); a panic inside an install at run time (the guard is unit-tested); WebKit's layout of the real UI (the screenshots are Chromium); Settings → Profile's package sentence inside a real instance UI (it is a template branch, typechecked only).
